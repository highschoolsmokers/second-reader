# Outline — Maxwell × Bruner

Braided-argument structure. ~8,000 words total, roughly distributed below.

## I. Opening move (~800 words)

- **Hook**: the Giacometti sculpture scene — the narrator standing in the Museum of Modern Art, recognizing his own reconstructive practice in the open-cage figures of *Palace at 4 A.M.*
- **Stakes**: what the essay will settle — that Maxwell's formal hesitations are the working apparatus of a theory of self-making, and that Bruner's narrative psychology gives us the best vocabulary for it.
- Land on thesis by the end of the section.

## II. The framework: Bruner, briefly (~1,000 words)

- The move from the cognitive-revolution Bruner (1960s) to the narrative-turn Bruner (*Acts of Meaning*, 1990; *Making Stories*, 2002).
- Three concepts the essay will actually use: **subjunctivizing reality**, **folk psychology as narrative**, **the self as a distributed, transactional construction** (not a retrievable fact).
- Where Bruner sits relative to Ricoeur and Eakin — enough to justify the choice, not a full survey.

## III. First pressure point: the Giacometti as narrative thesis (~1,500 words)

- Close reading of the Giacometti passage.
- How the narrator explicitly offers the sculpture as a model for what the book is doing.
- The match with Bruner's "distributed, perspectival" self: the open structure, the absent figures, the scaffolding that's part of the object.
- First place the text complicates the framework — the narrator's aestheticizing of his own guilt.

## IV. Second pressure point: the counterfactuals and the subjunctive mode (~1,500 words)

- The chapters that imagine Cletus's and Lloyd Wilson's inner lives.
- Bruner on the subjunctive: narrative traffics in what *might have been*, what *might yet be* — not in fact-retrieval.
- Why this is not evasion (the trauma-theory reading) but cognitive-moral work.
- The risk Maxwell runs — the ethics of novelizing real people's interiors (the Pratt case material).

## V. Third pressure point: the missed greeting and the limits of narrative repair (~1,500 words)

- The school-corridor scene as the novel's traumatic core.
- Bruner's optimism about narrative-as-repair meets Maxwell's refusal of full repair.
- What the novel stages that Bruner's framework doesn't fully cover: the places where narrative self-making *acknowledges* its own insufficiency.
- This is the section that pressures framework hardest.

## VI. Synthesis (~1,200 words)

- What the three pressure points say together:
  - Giacometti → self as distributed construction
  - Counterfactuals → subjunctive as moral instrument
  - Missed greeting → narrative repair has edges
- The composite: Bruner gives us a criticism that treats form as folk psychology in action, but Maxwell also shows us what that theory has to learn from a novelist who's taken its implications seriously.
- Brief comparative gesture — what this gains over trauma-theory or affect-theory readings.

## VII. Closing (~500 words)

- Return to the Giacometti.
- Land the thesis as earned: the novel is not about memory, it is *doing* memory — and the doing is Brunerian in a way we can now see clearly.
- Final note on stakes for literary studies and for Bruner's reception.
